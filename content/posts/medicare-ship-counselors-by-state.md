Free, unbiased help with Medicare exists in every single state, and most people have no idea it's available. The program is called SHIP, which stands for State Health Insurance Assistance Program. SHIP counselors are trained volunteers and staff who sit down with you (or talk by phone) and help you sort through Medicare's options at absolutely no cost and with no product to sell. If you've been trying to compare Part D drug plans on your own at 11 p.m. and want to throw your laptop across the room, this is who you should be calling instead.

Every state, plus Washington D.C., Puerto Rico, Guam, and the U.S. Virgin Islands, has its own SHIP office funded through a federal grant administered by the Administration for Community Living. The names vary by state, which trips people up. In California it's called HICAP (Health Insurance Counseling and Advocacy Program). In New York it's HIICAP. In Florida it's SHINE (Serving Health Insurance Needs of Elders). Same federal program, different local branding. When you search your state, look for both "SHIP" and your state's local name.

## What SHIP Counselors Actually Do

The scope here is wider than most people expect. A counselor can walk you through your [Initial Enrollment Period](/medicare-initial-enrollment-period-when-to-sign-up-at-65/) when you first become eligible, help you compare Medicare Advantage (Part C) plans against Original Medicare plus a Medigap supplement, and run the numbers on Part D drug formularies based on your specific prescriptions. They can also help you apply for the Extra Help program (also called the Low Income Subsidy, or LIS), which can reduce your drug costs significantly if your income and assets are within the qualifying limits.

Where SHIP counselors really shine, and where you won't get this from an insurance agent, is dispute resolution. If Medicare has denied a claim you think should be covered, a counselor can explain your appeal rights and walk you through the five-level [Medicare appeals process](/medicare-appeals-process/). They can help you file a complaint with your State Insurance Department or contact a Medicare Administrative Contractor (MAC) about a billing error. This kind of advocacy work is completely outside what any broker or plan representative is going to do for you.

They are not licensed agents. They won't enroll you in a plan on the spot, and they won't push any particular insurer. Some people find this limiting. But if you want an objective second opinion before you sign up for anything, there is no better resource.

## How to Find Your State's SHIP Office

The fastest route is **shiphelp.org**, which is the official national SHIP website. Click "Find Local Help," enter your [zip code](/medicare-plan-finder-by-zip-code/), and you'll get the direct phone number and address for your local SHIP office. You can also reach SHIP through:

- **1-800-MEDICARE** (1-800-633-4227): Tell the representative you want to reach your State Health Insurance Assistance Program. They can transfer you or give you the number.
- **Your State's Department of Insurance or Aging website**: Search "[your state] SHIP Medicare counseling." Most state aging offices have a direct SHIP page.
- **Eldercare Locator**: Call 1-800-677-1116 or visit eldercare.acl.gov. This is a free federal service that connects older adults to local resources, including SHIP.

One thing that can go wrong: you might reach a statewide SHIP hotline and get put on a callback list during busy periods, especially in October and November when Open Enrollment is underway. Call earlier in the enrollment season if you can. Some states have walk-in appointments at senior centers, libraries, or Area Agencies on Aging (AAAs), which can be faster than waiting for a phone callback.

## When You Should Call SHIP

Timing matters. The moments where SHIP counseling gives you the most value are:

**Three to four months before you turn 65.** Your Initial Enrollment Period (IEP) opens three months before the month you turn 65, includes your birthday month, and extends three months after. Getting counseling before it starts means you can avoid the most expensive mistake in Medicare: missing your enrollment window and triggering a late enrollment penalty. The Part B late penalty, for example, adds 10% to your premium permanently for each 12-month period you were eligible but didn't enroll. That adds up over decades.

**Before October 15 each year.** Open Enrollment runs October 15 through December 7, and any plan you pick takes effect January 1. Drug formularies change, premiums change, and the plan that worked for you last year may no longer cover your medications at the same tier. SHIP counselors can run a plan comparison using the Medicare Plan Finder at Medicare.gov, but with a human being who can explain what the results mean.

**After a major health or life change.** A new diagnosis, a move to a different state, or a change in employer coverage can all trigger a Special Enrollment Period (SEP). These are time-sensitive, with windows that often close after just 60 days, and they come with specific rules about what documentation you need. A SHIP counselor can tell you exactly which SEP applies to you and what paperwork to gather.

**After a denied claim.** Don't wait on this one. Medicare appeal deadlines are strict. A denied Medicare Advantage claim, for example, typically must be appealed within 65 days of the denial notice. SHIP counselors can help you draft an appeal letter and understand what standard of review applies.

**Example:** Consider someone who turns 65 in March 2027. Their IEP opens December 1, 2026 and runs through June 30, 2027 (three months before the birthday month, the birthday month itself, and three months after). If they call SHIP in November 2026, a counselor can explain whether their current employer coverage qualifies them to delay Part B without penalty, what their Part D options are, and how to compare a Medigap Plan G against a Medicare Advantage plan given their current prescriptions and doctors. Getting this advice in November leaves plenty of time to make an informed decision before the IEP window closes.

## A Note on SHIP Funding and Capacity

SHIP offices are federally funded but locally staffed, often heavily by volunteers. The [Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services](https://www.cms.gov/) and the Administration for Community Living publish annual data showing how many counseling sessions SHIP programs deliver nationally. The numbers run into the millions of contacts per year, which tells you the demand is real. Some state programs are better resourced than others. If you live in a rural area, your nearest in-person SHIP counselor might be an hour away, but phone appointments are almost always available.

AARP's Medicare resource center at [aarp.org/health/medicare-insurance](https://www.aarp.org/health/medicare-insurance/) also has plain-language explainers on Medicare that complement what SHIP offers, though AARP's counseling comes with its own insurance products nearby. That's not a reason to avoid their educational content, but it's worth knowing the difference between SHIP's fully independent counseling and resources that come from an organization with an insurance arm.
