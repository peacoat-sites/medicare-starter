The official numbers are finally here. On November 17, 2026, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) released the 2027 Medicare cost figures, and the timing couldn't be tighter: the [Annual Enrollment Period](/medicare-annual-enrollment-period-dates-2026/) (AEP) closes on December 7, just 20 days away. If you're still comparing plans or wondering whether to switch, these are the real numbers you've been waiting for.

The headline is reasonably good news. The standard [Medicare Part](/best-medicare-part-d-plans-2026/) B premium, which covers outpatient care, doctor visits, and preventive services, rises to $209.40 per month in 2027, up $6.50 from $202.90 this year. That's a 3.2% increase, and it's a noticeable contrast to the $17.90 jump absorbed for 2026. AARP called it only a slight increase, and that framing feels accurate. Still, "smaller than last year" doesn't mean free, and there are some other cost changes that deserve a closer look before December 7 arrives.

## What the Part B Changes Actually Mean for Your Budget

The Part B deductible, the amount you pay out of pocket before Medicare starts covering your share, rises $9 to $292 in 2027, up from $283 this year. Put the two changes together and a standard enrollee faces $87 more in total annual Part B costs. That's about $7.25 extra [per month](/average-medicare-costs-per-month/). Not painful for most people, but real money nonetheless, especially for those on a fixed income.

As the CMS fact sheet published November 17 makes clear, these standard figures apply to most Medicare beneficiaries. But "most" isn't everyone, and that distinction matters a lot depending on your income.

## The IRMAA Factor: Higher-Income Beneficiaries Pay Significantly More

IRMAA stands for Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount. It's a surcharge added to your Part B premium if your modified adjusted gross income exceeds certain thresholds, based on your tax return from two years prior. So your 2027 premium is determined by your 2025 income.

The surcharges are steep at the top. In 2027, the highest IRMAA tier adds $502.40 per month to the standard premium, bringing the total Part B cost to $711.80 per month for those enrollees. That's nearly $8,542 per year for Part B alone. Even the first IRMAA tier adds a meaningful amount, so if your income was above $106,000 as an individual or $212,000 as a couple in 2025, check your tier carefully. Medicare.gov has a tool to help you estimate where you land.

| Income (Individual, 2025) | Income (Married Filing Jointly, 2025) | 2027 Monthly Part B Premium |
|---|---|---|
| $106,000 or less | $212,000 or less | $209.40 |
| $106,001 to $133,000 | $212,001 to $266,000 | $300.90 |
| $133,001 to $167,000 | $266,001 to $334,000 | $392.40 |
| $167,001 to $200,000 | $334,001 to $400,000 | $483.90 |
| Above $200,000 | Above $400,000 | $711.80 |

*Note: CMS releases exact IRMAA bracket thresholds separately; figures above reflect the standard and top tiers confirmed in the November 17 CMS fact sheet. Confirm your specific tier at Medicare.gov.*

## Part D Drug Coverage: The Changes That Might Surprise You

Part D covers prescription drugs, either through a standalone plan or through a Medicare Advantage plan that includes drug coverage. The 2027 numbers here are worth your attention.

The standard Part D deductible rises to $700 in 2027. That's an $85 jump, and it's the figure Kiplinger flagged as one of the more significant cost shifts for the coming year. Not every Part D plan charges the full deductible, but many do, particularly for brand-name drugs. If you're on expensive medications, compare deductible structures carefully before December 7.

The annual out-of-pocket cap for Part D also increases to $2,400 in 2027. This cap was introduced as part of the Inflation Reduction Act reforms and is genuinely protective for people with high drug costs. Even at $2,400, it's a ceiling that many beneficiaries in past years never had. If your medications are costly, hitting the cap means Medicare picks up 100% of covered drug costs for the rest of the year.

## Part A: The Hospital Coverage Numbers

Part A covers inpatient hospital stays, skilled nursing facility care, and hospice. Most people don't pay a monthly premium for Part A if they or their spouse worked and paid Medicare taxes for at least 10 years. But the inpatient deductible, what you owe per benefit period if you're admitted, does change.

In 2027, the Part A inpatient deductible rises to $1,776 per benefit period. A benefit period starts the day you're admitted and ends after you've been out of the hospital or skilled nursing facility for 60 consecutive days. It's not an annual deductible; you could technically owe it more than once in a year if you have separate hospital stays.

| Cost Type | 2026 Amount | 2027 Amount | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Part B Monthly Premium (standard) | $202.90 | $209.40 | +$6.50 |
| Part B Annual Deductible | $283 | $292 | +$9 |
| Part D Standard Deductible | $615 | $700 | +$85 |
| Part D Out-of-Pocket Cap | $2,100 | $2,400 | +$300 |
| Part A Inpatient Deductible | $1,736 | $1,776 | +$40 |

## What to Do Before December 7

You have until December 7 at midnight to enroll in, switch, or drop a Medicare Advantage (Part C) or Part D plan. Coverage from any change made during the AEP takes effect January 1, 2027. With the 2027 cost figures now confirmed, you can compare plans with real numbers rather than estimates.

A few things worth checking in the final weeks: review your current plan's annual notice of change, which should have arrived in late September, since premiums, formularies (the list of covered drugs), and networks can all shift year to year. Use the Medicare Plan Finder at Medicare.gov to compare options in your ZIP code. And if your income changed significantly in 2025, consider whether IRMAA might affect you, or whether a previous surcharge might be appealed if your income dropped due to a qualifying life event.

A licensed State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) counselor can walk you through your options at no cost. Find your local SHIP at shiphelp.org.

These numbers are now official. The deadline is close. Taking an hour this week to review your plan against the confirmed 2027 costs could save you money for the entire coming year, and that's worth the time.
