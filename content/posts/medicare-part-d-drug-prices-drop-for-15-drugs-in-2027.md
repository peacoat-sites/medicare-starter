[Open enrollment](/medicare-open-enrollment-period/) closes December 7, 2026, and this year there's a genuinely big reason not to let that deadline slip by. Medicare has locked in negotiated prices for 15 drugs that take effect January 1, 2027, including a 71% price cut on Ozempic, Rybelsus, and Wegovy. If you or someone you love takes any of these medications, the plan you pick right now will determine how much of those savings actually reach your wallet.

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, known as CMS, struck agreements on all 15 drugs in its second negotiation cycle. Price reductions across the group range from 38% to 85% off 2024 list prices, according to CMS negotiated price data published in October 2026. The semaglutide drugs, Ozempic and Rybelsus for type 2 diabetes and Wegovy for weight management, will carry a Medicare negotiated price of $274 for a 30-day supply, down from a 2024 list price of $959. CMS projects these new prices will save Part D ([prescription drug](/how-to-choose-a-medicare-part-d-prescription-drug-plan/) coverage) enrollees about $685 million in out-of-pocket costs in 2027. That's real money. But the negotiated price is only part of the story, and this is the part many people miss during open enrollment.

## What the Negotiated Prices Actually Are

The Medicare [drug price negotiation](/medicare-drug-price-negotiation-heads-to-2028-what-beneficiaries-shoul/) program was created under the Inflation Reduction Act, and 2027 marks only the second year it's producing real results at the pharmacy counter. CMS publishes a "maximum fair price" for each negotiated drug, and Part D plans are required to offer that drug at or below that price. The 71% reduction on Ozempic gets most of the headlines, and it deserves attention. But the full list covers medications used by millions of seniors across several serious conditions, including heart disease, blood thinners, and diabetes drugs beyond the semaglutide family.

The reductions across the 15 drugs vary considerably. Here's a snapshot of what the negotiated pricing structure looks like based on data from CMS and reporting by Qz.com:

| Drug | Primary Use | 2024 List Price (approx.) | 2027 Negotiated Price | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ozempic / Rybelsus / Wegovy | Type 2 diabetes / Weight management | $959/month | $274/month | 71% |
| Range across all 15 drugs | Various | Varies | Varies | 38%–85% |

That range from 38% to 85% matters. Some drugs in the group see more modest cuts; others see even deeper reductions than Ozempic. If you take any medication that might appear on this list, check Medicare.gov or call 1-800-MEDICARE to confirm whether your specific drug is included.

## Why Your Plan's Formulary Still Matters

Here's where seniors can get tripped up. The negotiated price is a ceiling, not a universal copay. Each Part D plan places drugs on its own formulary, which is a list of covered drugs organized into pricing tiers. A plan might place Ozempic on Tier 3 or Tier 4, meaning your actual cost-sharing could be a flat copay or a percentage of the drug's cost, not simply $274. Some plans require prior authorization or step therapy (where you try a different drug first) before they'll cover a drug at all.

This means two people on the same drug, both enrolled in Medicare Part D, could pay very different amounts depending on the specific plan they choose. Comparing plans on Medicare.gov's Plan Finder tool, using your actual list of prescriptions, is the most reliable way to see your projected annual cost under each plan. The Plan Finder calculates your estimated total drug costs, including premiums, deductibles, and copays, so you can make a side-by-side comparison. Don't just look at the monthly premium. A plan with a lower premium sometimes costs more overall if your drugs sit on expensive tiers.

## The Premium and Deductible Changes Coming in 2027

The good news about negotiated drug prices shares the stage with some less comfortable news. The average monthly standalone Part D premium is expected to jump from $34 in 2026 to roughly $51 in 2027, an increase of about 50%. That increase is tied in part to the expiration of a federal premium stabilization subsidy that had been holding premiums down. As Yahoo Finance reported in November 2026, this is one of the most significant Part D premium increases in recent years, and it catches many people off guard.

The standard Part D deductible ceiling also rises, from $615 in 2026 to $700 in 2027. And the annual out-of-pocket cap, the most you'd pay for covered drugs in a year, moves up from $2,100 to $2,400. That cap is still a meaningful protection; before 2025, there was no cap at all (it began at $2,000 in 2025). But it's worth knowing the number has changed. If you're someone who hits the cap most years because of high-cost medications, $2,400 is your 2027 target to plan around.

| Cost Feature | 2026 | 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Average standalone Part D premium | ~$34/month | ~$51/month |
| Standard deductible ceiling | $615 | $700 |
| Annual out-of-pocket cap | $2,100 | $2,400 |

## What to Do Before December 7

Open enrollment runs through December 7, 2026. If you do nothing, you'll stay in your current plan, which may or may not be the right choice given these changes. Your current plan might have adjusted its formulary, its premiums, or its tier placement for 2027.

Start at Medicare.gov and use the Plan Finder with your full medication list. If you take one of the 15 negotiated drugs, specifically check how each plan covers it, what tier it's on, and whether prior authorization is required. Also factor in the new premium range. A plan that looked affordable at $34 a month may now cost $51 or more, and a competing plan might offer better overall value.

If the online tools feel overwhelming, free help is available through SHIP, the State Health Insurance Assistance Program. SHIP counselors are trained volunteers and staff who can walk through plan options with you at no cost. Find your local SHIP at shiphelp.org or call 1-800-MEDICARE. It's also reasonable to consult a licensed Medicare insurance broker, particularly if you take multiple specialty medications.

The savings from negotiated drug prices are real, and $685 million in projected out-of-pocket relief is not a small number. Whether those savings reach you in 2027 depends on the plan you're enrolled in come January 1. Take the time now, before December 7, to make sure your plan is working as hard as you are.
