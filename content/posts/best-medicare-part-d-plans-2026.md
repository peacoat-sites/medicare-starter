You didn't change anything. The plan did. And now you have about six weeks to figure out what to do about it. I've seen this exact situation play out hundreds of times, and every single time the person at that kitchen table says the same thing: "I didn't know I could switch."

You can switch. Every year. And for 2026, there are real reasons to pay attention during Open Enrollment, which runs October 15 through December 7, 2025.

## What Part D Actually Is (And Why the Details Matter More Than the Premium)

Part D is Medicare's prescription drug coverage. It's sold by private insurance companies that are approved by Medicare, and it comes in two forms: a standalone Prescription Drug Plan (PDP) that pairs with Original Medicare (Parts A and B), or as part of a Medicare Advantage plan (sometimes called Part C or MA-PD) that bundles drug coverage with your medical benefits.

Here's what most people don't realize: the monthly premium is almost never the most important number. A plan with a $0 premium can cost you thousands more per year than a plan with a $35 premium, depending on which drugs you take and how that plan's formulary is structured. A formulary is the list of drugs a plan covers, and each drug gets assigned to a tier that determines your cost. Lower tiers mean lower copays. Your medication might be on Tier 2 in one plan and Tier 4 in another, and that difference adds up fast.

The 2026 plan year also comes with an important milestone. Thanks to the Inflation Reduction Act, the out-of-pocket cap on Part D drug costs is now $2,000 per year. Before this law passed, there was no true cap, and some people with serious illnesses were spending $5,000, $7,000, or more annually just on prescriptions. That $2,000 ceiling is a meaningful protection, and it's now standard on every Part D plan. Medicare.gov has a full breakdown of how this cap works and what counts toward it.

## The Big Changes Heading Into 2026

Every fall, Medicare releases updated plan data and the landscape shifts. Premiums change. Formularies get restructured. Some plans enter a market, others leave. Going into 2026, here's what you need to know.

The national benchmark premium that determines whether you owe a Late Enrollment Penalty is recalculated each year. For 2026, that number is expected to shift modestly, though final figures are published by CMS (the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services) in the fall. What doesn't change is the penalty itself: if you delayed enrolling in Part D when you were first eligible and didn't have other creditable drug coverage, you pay a permanent penalty added to your premium for as long as you have Part D.

Plans are also adjusting to the new cost-sharing structure created by the Inflation Reduction Act. The "coverage gap" (the old "donut hole") has been eliminated in its traditional form, replaced with a simpler three-phase structure: deductible phase, initial coverage phase, and catastrophic phase. With the $2,000 cap in place, most people with high drug costs will reach catastrophic coverage and pay nothing for the rest of the year. This is genuinely good news, but it also means plan premiums have risen in some markets to offset the insurers' increased costs.

One more thing to watch: Medicare is offering a new option called the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan (sometimes called the "smoothing" option), which lets you spread your drug costs across the year in monthly installments rather than paying large lump sums. This doesn't reduce what you owe, but it can prevent situations where you hit a large bill in January and struggle to cover it. You have to opt in each year through your plan.

## How to Actually Find the Best Part D Plan for Your Situation

There's no single "best" Part D plan. That's not a cop-out. It's the truth. The best plan for you depends entirely on your specific medications, the pharmacies you use, and where you live. Plans are regional, so a plan that's excellent in Florida may not even be available in Oregon.

Here's the step-by-step process I recommend:

**Step 1: Make your medication list.** Write down every prescription drug you take, including the exact name (brand or generic), the dosage, and how many times per day you take it. Don't guess. Pull your pharmacy receipts or call your pharmacy.

**Step 2: Go to Medicare's Plan Finder.** The tool is at [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/) and it's genuinely useful once you know how to use it. Enter your zip code, your medications, and your preferred pharmacy. The tool will show you every plan available in your area and calculate your estimated annual drug cost under each one, including premiums, deductibles, and copays.

**Step 3: Sort by estimated annual cost, not just premium.** This is where people make the most expensive mistakes. Always look at the total estimated yearly cost column. A plan with a $45 premium might estimate $600 in total drug costs for the year. A plan with a $12 premium might estimate $1,400. The math isn't complicated once you see the numbers side by side.

**Step 4: Check your pharmacy.** Make sure your preferred pharmacy is in-network as a preferred pharmacy, not just a standard in-network pharmacy. The cost difference between preferred and standard network pharmacies can be significant, sometimes $20 to $40 per prescription.

**Step 5: Look at the plan's Star Rating.** CMS rates every Medicare plan on a scale of 1 to 5 stars. Five is best. A plan rated below 3 stars consistently is a warning sign for poor customer service, claims handling, or member satisfaction. You can see Star Ratings on the Medicare Plan Finder.

**Step 6: Check what happens in January.** Even if your drugs are covered now, plans can make mid-year formulary changes with notice. Read the Annual Notice of Change (ANOC) that your current plan mails every September. It tells you exactly what's changing in the new year.

## What Makes a Part D Plan "Good" Beyond the Basics

A few things separate the genuinely solid plans from the ones that look good on paper.

**Formulary stability.** Some plans keep their formularies relatively stable year to year. Others restructure aggressively. While there's no perfect way to predict this, you can look at whether your specific drugs were covered by a plan in the prior year and how their tier placements compare.

**Exception and appeals process.** If a drug you need isn't on the formulary or is on a high tier, you have the right to request a formulary exception. Plans with efficient exception processes and responsive customer service are worth the investment. AARP's Medicare resource center at [aarp.org/health/medicare-insurance/](https://www.aarp.org/health/medicare-insurance/) has practical guidance on how to file an exception request if you ever need to go that route.

**Mail-order options.** Most Part D plans offer lower copays for a 90-day mail-order supply versus a 30-day retail supply. If you take maintenance medications (drugs you take every day for a chronic condition), this alone can save you hundreds of dollars a year.

**Coverage of specialty drugs.** If you take biologics, cancer drugs, or other specialty medications, the tier placement and prior authorization requirements for those drugs should be your first priority in comparing plans, not the premium.

## Extra Help: The Program Most People Don't Know to Ask About

If your income and resources are limited, you may qualify for Extra Help, officially called the Low Income Subsidy (LIS). This is a federal program that pays for most of your Part D costs, including premiums, deductibles, and copays. For 2025, individuals with income below roughly 150% of the federal poverty level may qualify, and the 2026 thresholds will be similar.

What I find heartbreaking is how many people are eligible and simply don't know it. The Social Security Administration estimates millions of people qualify for Extra Help but aren't enrolled. You apply through Social Security, either online at ssa.gov, by phone, or at your local Social Security office. If you qualify, you also get a Special Enrollment Period to switch Part D plans at any time during the year, which is a significant flexibility benefit.

Not sure whether you qualify? Your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) can help. SHIP counselors provide free, unbiased Medicare counseling. Find your local SHIP at shiphelp.org.

The honest truth is that reviewing your Part D coverage every year is one of the highest-value things you can do for your financial health. It takes about an hour using Medicare's Plan Finder, and the potential savings can be substantial. Plans change, your medications may change, and the plan that was perfect in 2024 may not be the best fit for 2026. Set a reminder now for mid-October, get your medication list ready, and give yourself that hour.
