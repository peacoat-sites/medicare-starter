## What Medicare Supplement Insurance Actually Is (and Why the Company Matters More Than You Think)

Medicare Supplement insurance, also called Medigap, is private insurance that wraps around Original Medicare (Parts A and B) to cover costs that Medicare leaves on your plate: things like deductibles, coinsurance, and copayments. The federal government standardizes the plans, meaning a Plan G from Company A covers exactly the same benefits as a Plan G from Company B. No exceptions.

So if the benefits are identical, why does the company still matter? A few reasons:

**Price.** Even for the exact same plan letter, premiums can vary by 50% or more between carriers in the same ZIP code. That gap can add up to thousands of dollars over a decade.

**Rate stability.** Some companies raise premiums aggressively every year. Others have quieter, more predictable increases. You won't find that in a brochure.

**Financial strength.** You need to know your insurer will pay claims 10 or 15 years from now, not just today.

**Customer service.** When you're dealing with a hospital bill at 67, you want someone to answer the phone and actually help.

Before I name names, let me give you the evaluation framework I've used with clients for years. Judging a Medigap carrier on reputation alone is a mistake. You want to look at four specific things.

**AM Best financial strength rating.** AM Best is an independent rating agency focused exclusively on insurance companies. You want a carrier rated A (Excellent) or better. An A- is fine. A B+ starts to raise questions for me, especially on a long-term policy.

**NAIC complaint ratio.** The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) publishes a complaint index for every insurer. A score of 1.0 is the industry median. Anything above 1.0 means the company receives more complaints than average for its size. Look this up at naic.org before you commit.

**Rate increase history.** Ask your agent or the company directly: what has the average annual premium increase been over the past five years for your specific plan in your state? Legitimate carriers will tell you. If they dodge the question, walk away.

**Plan availability in your state.** Not every company sells in every state, and some carriers are strong in certain regions and weak in others.

The [Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services](https://www.cms.gov/) publishes guidance on Medigap shopping that reinforces this multi-factor approach. Don't skip the research phase just because a company has a recognizable name.

## The Companies That Consistently Rank at the Top

I want to be honest here: there's no single "best" Medigap company for everyone. What I can tell you is which carriers show up repeatedly when you apply the framework above.

**AARP/UnitedHealthcare.** This is the largest Medigap insurer in the country by enrollment. The AARP name is the brand, but UnitedHealthcare underwrites the policies. They carry strong AM Best ratings, offer plans in all 50 states, and their household discounts (typically around 5-11%) can make them competitive on price. The trade-off: their base premiums run higher than some competitors in many markets. They use a community-rated pricing method in most states, which can actually work in your favor as you age.

**Mutual of Omaha.** This is the carrier I've seen win on price most often in mid-sized markets. They've been around since 1909, carry an A+ AM Best rating, and have a strong track record of customer service. Their Plan G and Plan N offerings are frequently among the most competitively priced. Household discounts are available in many states.

**Cigna.** Cigna doesn't dominate Medigap market share the way UnitedHealthcare does, but they've earned solid marks for financial strength and claim payment speed. In some states, their pricing is surprisingly competitive. Worth including in any quote comparison.

**Aetna.** Aetna has improved its Medigap offerings significantly in the past several years. They have a broad network presence and solid customer service scores. Like Cigna, pricing is highly regional, so they might be the best deal in your county or a middle-of-the-pack option in the next county over.

**Blue Cross Blue Shield (BCBS) affiliates.** "Blue Cross" isn't one company, it's a network of independent regional carriers. In some regions, the local BCBS plan is the dominant and most affordable Medigap option. In others, it's expensive. You have to check locally.

**Transamerica and GPM Life.** These names come up less often in mainstream articles, but I've seen them price very aggressively in rural markets and underserved states. If a quote comparison tool surfaces them, don't dismiss them. Verify the AM Best rating and move on from there.

## The Most Popular Plan Choices in 2025 and 2026

The plan letter you choose matters just as much as the company. Two plans dominate enrollment for new Medicare beneficiaries today.

**Plan G** is the most comprehensive option available to people who became Medicare-eligible after January 1, 2020. It covers nearly everything except the Part B deductible (which is $240 in 2024). If you want predictable costs and dislike surprise bills, Plan G is built for you. [Our full explanation of Medicare Supplement Plan G](/medicare-supplement-plan-g-explained/) covers every benefit in detail.

**Plan N** is the budget-friendly alternative. Premiums run 15-25% lower than Plan G in most markets, but you'll pay copayments of up to $20 for office visits and up to $50 for emergency room visits. You're also exposed to Part B excess charges, which some doctors (those who don't accept Medicare assignment) are allowed to tack on. If you're relatively healthy, see a moderate number of doctors, and your providers accept Medicare assignment, Plan N can be a smart financial choice. ## Step-by-Step: How to Actually Shop for the Best Company

Here's the practical process I walk clients through. It takes maybe two or three hours, and it's worth every minute.

**Step 1: Confirm your eligibility window.** Medigap Open Enrollment starts when you're 65 and enrolled in Part B. During this six-month window, no company can reject you or charge you more due to health conditions. This is the most valuable window in all of Medicare insurance. Don't miss it. Our [Medicare eligibility requirements](/medicare-eligibility-requirements/) guide explains exactly when this window opens.

**Step 2: Decide on the plan letter first.** Use the comparison table above to decide whether Plan G or Plan N fits your health situation and risk tolerance. Locking in the plan letter before comparing carriers keeps you from comparing apples to oranges.

**Step 3: Get quotes from at least five carriers.** Use Medicare.gov's Medigap plan finder, or work with an independent broker who represents multiple companies (not a captive agent who sells only one carrier). Independent brokers don't cost you extra. Their commission comes from the carrier.

**Step 4: Look up each carrier's NAIC complaint ratio.** Go to naic.org, find the "Insurance Company Search" tool, pull up each carrier, and look at their complaint index for the current year. Eliminate any carrier with a ratio above 1.5.

**Step 5: Verify the AM Best rating.** A quick search at ambest.com will confirm financial strength. Aim for A- or better.

**Step 6: Ask about rate increase history.** Call the carrier or ask your broker. Phrase it simply: "What has the average annual premium increase been for this plan in my state over the past five years?" Document the answer.

**Step 7: Apply during your Open Enrollment window.** Once you've selected a carrier and plan, apply promptly. Your guaranteed issue rights don't last forever.

AARP's Medicare resource center at [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/health/medicare-insurance/) also offers a quote comparison tool and educational content that can supplement this research process, particularly if you're comparing their UnitedHealthcare-backed plans to other options.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the cheapest Medigap company always a bad choice?

Not at all. Because Medigap benefits are federally standardized, a lower premium doesn't mean lower coverage. The same Plan G benefits apply regardless of what you pay. A lower-cost carrier can be the best choice if they have strong financial ratings and a reasonable complaint history. Price shop without guilt.

### Can a Medicare Supplement company drop me if I get sick?

No. As long as you pay your premiums on time, a Medigap insurer cannot cancel your policy due to health changes. This is a federal protection under guaranteed renewability rules. Your coverage can't be taken away because you develop diabetes, cancer, or any other condition.

### Do I need Medigap if I have retiree health insurance from a former employer?

Maybe not, but you need to understand how they coordinate before you make a decision. Employer retiree coverage and Medicare work together in specific ways. Our article on [Medicare and employer insurance](/medicare-and-employer-insurance/) explains exactly how that coordination works so you don't pay for duplicate coverage.

### How often can I switch Medicare Supplement companies?

You can apply to switch carriers at any time, but outside of certain protected situations, you'll need to pass medical underwriting. Insurers can reject you or charge higher premiums based on your health history. This is why choosing carefully at the start, during your Open Enrollment window, matters so much.

### Are Medicare Advantage plans a better deal than Medigap?

It depends entirely on your health, how much you travel, and how important predictable costs are to you. Medicare Advantage (Part C) plans often have lower or zero premiums but come with networks, referrals, and out-of-pocket maximums you need to understand carefully. Our [Medigap vs. Medicare Advantage](/medigap-vs-medicare-advantage/) article lays out both sides honestly so you can make the comparison yourself.

There's no magic list that tells you which Medicare Supplement company is best for you. But there's absolutely a right process, and following it means you won't overpay, won't end up with a carrier that fights your claims, and won't be caught off guard by runaway rate increases five years from now. Take the time. Use the framework. And if you feel overwhelmed, sit down with an independent Medicare broker who can walk through your specific situation without a sales agenda.
