Every fall, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) announces the next year's standard Medicare costs. The 2027 amounts came out on November 17, 2026. Here they are next to 2026, so you can see what went up and by how much.

## What these numbers actually mean

**Part B premium ($209.40/month in 2027).** This is the standard monthly premium most people pay for Part B, which covers doctor visits, outpatient care and medical equipment. It rose $6.50 from 2026, about 3.2%, a much smaller increase than the $17.90 jump the year before (from $185.00 in 2025 to $202.90 in 2026). Most people have it taken out of their Social Security check. Details are in our guide to [Medicare Part B costs](/medicare-part-b-premium/).

**Part B deductible ($292 in 2027).** You pay this once a year before Part B starts paying its share. After that, you typically pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount for most services.

**Part A is free for most people.** If you or your spouse paid Medicare taxes for at least 40 quarters (about 10 years of work), you pay **$0** for Part A. If not, the 2027 premium is $328 a month with 30 to 39 quarters, or $597 a month with fewer than 30.

**The Part A hospital deductible ($1,776 in 2027) is per benefit period, not per year.** A benefit period starts the day you're admitted and ends after you've been out of the hospital (or skilled nursing care) for 60 days in a row. Two hospital stays in one year with more than 60 days between them can mean paying the deductible twice. Days 61 through 90 of a stay cost $444 a day in 2027, and lifetime reserve days cost $888 a day.

**Higher earners pay a surcharge (IRMAA).** For 2027, the surcharge starts when your 2025 modified adjusted gross income is above $112,000 on a single return or $224,000 on a joint return, and Part B can reach $711.80 a month. See the full [2027 IRMAA brackets](/irmaa-medicare-surcharge/) or estimate yours with the [IRMAA calculator](/irmaa-medicare-calculator/).

## How Part B costs have changed year by year

Looking at one year hides the bigger story: the standard Part B premium has roughly doubled since 2015, from $104.90 to $209.40. Here is the full run of official CMS figures.

| Year | Part B premium | Change | Part B deductible |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2015 | $104.90 | n/a | $147 |
| 2016 | $121.80 | +16.1% | $166 |
| 2017 | $134.00 | +10.0% | $183 |
| 2018 | $134.00 | 0% | $183 |
| 2019 | $135.50 | +1.1% | $185 |
| 2020 | $144.60 | +6.7% | $198 |
| 2021 | $148.50 | +2.7% | $203 |
| 2022 | $170.10 | +14.5% | $233 |
| 2023 | $164.90 | -3.1% | $226 |
| 2024 | $174.70 | +5.9% | $240 |
| 2025 | $185.00 | +5.9% | $257 |
| 2026 | $202.90 | +9.7% | $283 |
| 2027 | $209.40 | +3.2% | $292 |

Two years stand out. The 14.5% jump in 2022 was driven largely by CMS pricing in a controversial (and expensive) Alzheimer's drug, Aduhelm, that Medicare later restricted. That is also why 2023 brought the only premium decrease in over a decade: the Aduhelm money was given back.

The Part A hospital deductible follows the same climb:

| Year | Part A deductible (per benefit period) |
| --- | --- |
| 2020 | $1,408 |
| 2021 | $1,484 |
| 2022 | $1,556 |
| 2023 | $1,600 |
| 2024 | $1,632 |
| 2025 | $1,676 |
| 2026 | $1,736 |
| 2027 | $1,776 |

**What this means for planning.** From 2015 to 2027 the Part B premium grew about 6% a year on average, well ahead of general inflation over that stretch. The 2027 increase was smaller, but one calm year doesn't change the long-run pattern. If you're budgeting for retirement, assume Medicare costs can outpace your Social Security cost-of-living raise in some years. It's one of the main reasons to compare Medigap and Medicare Advantage options each fall instead of letting coverage ride.

## Where to get free, unbiased help

Costs are only one piece of the Medicare decision, and the right choice depends on your health, budget and the plans available where you live. For **free, one-on-one counseling**, contact your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP); every state has one. You can also call **1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227)**.
